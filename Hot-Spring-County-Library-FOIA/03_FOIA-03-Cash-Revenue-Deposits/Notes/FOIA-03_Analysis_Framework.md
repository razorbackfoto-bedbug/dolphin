# FOIA-03: Cash, Revenue, and Deposits Analysis Framework

## Purpose

Reconcile money received against deposits, bank statements, and revenue ledgers. Verify that all cash receipts are properly documented, deposited, and recorded.

## Reconciliation Strategy

The fundamental cash reconciliation:

**Cash Log → Deposit Slip → Bank Statement → Revenue Ledger**

For each deposit period (daily or weekly):

1. **Cash Log:** Sum of all cash received
2. **Deposit Slip:** Deposit amount to bank
3. **Bank Statement:** Confirmation of deposit to account
4. **Revenue Ledger:** Recording of receipt in accounting records

These should match. Discrepancies require explanation.

## Key Documents to Organize

### Daily Cash Records
- Date
- Opening balance (if any)
- Revenue source (fines, fees, donations, etc.)
- Amount received
- Disbursements (if any)
- Closing balance or amount available for deposit
- Reconciliation notes

### Deposits
- Date
- Amount
- Account deposited to
- Deposit slip or bank confirmation
- Reconciling detail (which receipts/sources included in this deposit)

### Revenue by Source
- Fines and late fees: daily or periodic totals
- Printing/copying: daily or periodic totals
- Room rental: charges and collections
- Book sales: proceeds
- Donations: amounts and dates
- Grants: award amounts and payment dates
- Other: clearly identified

## What to Track

Create a cash reconciliation summary by week or month:

| Period | Cash Receipts | Deposit Amount | Deposit Date | Bank Statement Confirmation | Ledger Entry | Variance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Red Flags in Cash and Revenue Records

Watch for (document but do not assume misconduct):

- Daily cash log not matching deposit slip amount
- Deposit delayed several days after receipts
- Missing daily cash logs for certain dates or periods
- Cash receipts not deposited immediately
- Deposits that do not correspond to cash log entries
- Missing receipt documentation for claimed revenues
- Cash shortage or overage without explanation
- Donations or gifts with unusual restrictions not documented
- Grant receipts without corresponding award documentation
- Fundraising proceeds without supporting event records
- Revenue ledger entries not matching deposit slips
- Revenue ledger entries not matching bank deposits
- Unexplained gaps in receipt numbers
- Round-dollar amounts suggesting estimates rather than actual receipts
- Missing bank statements for certain periods

## Analysis Questions

For each revenue receipt and deposit:

1. **Source:** What is the source of the receipt? Is it legitimate library revenue?
2. **Documentation:** Is there a receipt or documentation of the transaction?
3. **Amount:** Is the amount reasonable for the source?
4. **Deposit:** Was the money deposited promptly?
5. **Matching:** Does the deposit match the documented receipts?
6. **Recording:** Is the receipt recorded in the revenue ledger?
7. **Timing:** Is the ledger entry timely?
8. **Bank Confirmation:** Does the bank statement confirm the deposit?
9. **Reconciliation:** Is any variance between cash log and deposit explained?

## Analysis Checklist

- [ ] All daily cash logs obtained for the period
- [ ] All cash log entries summed by week and month
- [ ] All deposit slips obtained
- [ ] Deposits matched to corresponding cash logs
- [ ] All deposits confirmed in bank statements
- [ ] All deposits recorded in revenue ledger
- [ ] All revenue sources documented
- [ ] Revenue totals reconciled between sources
- [ ] Unusual revenues investigated (grants, donations, fundraising)
- [ ] Deposits and ledger entries reconciled
- [ ] Gaps in daily logs identified
- [ ] Timing of deposits reviewed (immediate vs. delayed)
- [ ] Revenue ledger reconciled to bank statement
- [ ] Donations or restricted funds tracked

## Foundation Fund Deposits

Note: If the library maintains a separate Foundation Fund account, track deposits to that account separately from operating account deposits. This will be addressed in more detail in FOIA-04.

## Follow-Up Items

After analyzing FOIA-03 responses, investigate:

- Any variance between cash logs and deposits
- Donations or grants with unusual timing or amounts
- Missing cash logs for specific dates
- Deposits not promptly recorded in the ledger
- Revenues inconsistent with library operations
- Delayed deposits requiring explanation
