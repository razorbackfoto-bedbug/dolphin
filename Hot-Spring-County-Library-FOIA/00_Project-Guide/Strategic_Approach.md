# Strategic Approach to FOIA Investigation

## Fundamental Principles

### 1. **No Accusations**
This FOIA project does not accuse anyone of misconduct. The purpose is to obtain existing public records and review them objectively.

### 2. **Neutrality**
All requests are framed neutrally, focusing on obtaining standard financial and operational records. They do not:
- Mention suspected financial malfeasance
- Reference the Friends of the Library dispute
- Discuss proposed MOUs or policy changes
- Raise theories about financial wrongdoing
- Reference other libraries or criminal cases
- Use inflammatory or accusatory language

### 3. **Factual Focus**
Requests seek existing public records only. They do not ask the agency to:
- Create new reports
- Conduct special analyses
- Compile data from multiple sources
- Perform calculations
- Render legal opinions

### 4. **Cross-Checking**
The requests are designed to enable verification of transactions across multiple documents:
- Receipts vs. invoices vs. ledgers vs. bank statements
- Cash logs vs. deposits vs. revenue records
- Board approvals vs. actual spending
- Budgets vs. actual expenditures
- Policies vs. actual practice

## Staged Approach

### Stage 1: Financial Baseline (FOIA-01 through FOIA-05)

Submit five initial requests simultaneously or in rapid succession:
- **FOIA-01:** General ledgers, budgets, financial statements
- **FOIA-02:** Credit cards, debit cards, receipts, reimbursements
- **FOIA-03:** Cash, revenue, deposits
- **FOIA-04:** Foundation Fund separate records
- **FOIA-05:** Policies, controls, board oversight

**Timeline:** All requests should be submitted within 1-2 weeks.

**Purpose:** Obtain comprehensive financial records and policies to establish baseline understanding of library finances.

**Stop Point:** After all five responses are received, stop and analyze before proceeding.

### Stage 2: Follow-Up Requests (After Initial Analysis)

Only after analyzing FOIA-01 through FOIA-05 responses, draft:
- **FOIA-06:** Targeted vendor and transaction follow-ups
- **FOIA-07:** Targeted communications
- **FOIA-08:** Friends of the Library records (if relevant)

**Key:** These are only drafted after specific issues are identified.

## What Success Looks Like

Successful completion of this project means:

1. **Records Obtained:** All five Stage 1 requests are responded to (completely or with identified exemptions)

2. **Records Organized:** Responses are properly received, organized, and cross-referenced

3. **Records Analyzed:** Initial responses are reviewed and key findings documented

4. **Discrepancies Identified:** Any gaps between financial records, policies, and board oversight are noted

5. **Evidence Logged:** All transactions are cataloged in the Evidence Log

6. **Recommendations:** Follow-up requests are drafted for specific issues requiring clarification

## What to Avoid

### In FOIA Requests (Public Facing):
- ❌ Accusations of misconduct
- ❌ Speculation or theories
- ❌ Inflammatory language
- ❌ References to other libraries or criminal cases
- ❌ Discussions of disputes or political context
- ❌ Requests for the agency to analyze or investigate
- ❌ Requests to create new records

### In Internal Notes:
- ❌ Do not include suspicions in documents intended for FOIA submission
- ❌ Keep analytical notes in the "Notes" folder, not the "Final-Request" folder
- ❌ Maintain professional separation between public requests and internal analysis

## Who Can Submit

This FOIA project is designed to be submitted by **a third party who is not connected to the Friends of the Library or the library itself.**

This neutral stance is important because:
- It avoids accusations of bias or self-interest
- It maintains professional credibility
- It prevents the library from dismissing the requests as politically motivated
- It allows the records to "speak for themselves"

## Records to Expect

### From FOIA-01 (Financial Baseline)
- Budgets (1-2 per year)
- Ledgers (may be monthly or annual compilations)
- Financial statements (monthly, quarterly, annual)
- Bank account lists
- Check registers (monthly pages)

**Volume Estimate:** 50-200 pages for 3 years of records

### From FOIA-02 (Cards and Purchases)
- Credit card statements (monthly, per cardholder)
- Debit card statements (monthly, per cardholder)
- Receipts (quantity: number of transactions)
- Invoices (quantity: vendor invoices)

**Volume Estimate:** 200-500 pages

### From FOIA-03 (Cash and Revenue)
- Daily cash logs (quantity: business days for 3 years)
- Deposit slips (quantity: deposits made)
- Revenue documentation by source

**Volume Estimate:** 100-300 pages

### From FOIA-04 (Foundation Fund)
- Bank statements (monthly or quarterly)
- Ledgers (annual or periodic)
- Financial statements (annual)
- Board documentation (as applicable)

**Volume Estimate:** 50-150 pages

### From FOIA-05 (Policies and Controls)
- Policies (1-20 depending on documentation practices)
- Board minutes (for meetings where financials discussed)
- Audit reports (annual or biennial)

**Volume Estimate:** 50-200 pages

**Total Estimated Records:** 450-1,350 pages

## Key Success Factors

1. **Professionalism:** Keep all public-facing requests professional, neutral, and focused on public records

2. **Persistence:** Follow up on incomplete responses, requests for fee estimates, and extended timelines

3. **Organization:** Implement the folder structure and tracking sheets immediately

4. **Completeness:** Request all necessary supporting documents the first time (receipts, invoices, approvals)

5. **Documentation:** Record all responses, fees, exemptions claimed, and timeline in the tracking sheet

6. **Analysis:** Take time to analyze the records thoroughly before deciding on follow-up requests

7. **Separation:** Keep public requests and internal analysis completely separate
